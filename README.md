### What Does “Connection” Mean?

This is one of those IPTV words that sounds more complicated than it is.

According to TiviLive, a connection is the number of screens that can stream **at the same time**. One connection means one active stream; multi-connection plans are intended for households with multiple viewers or devices.

So, for example, owning five TVs does not automatically mean you need five connections. The key question is whether multiple screens will actually be streaming simultaneously.

## How to Set Up IPTV After You Buy

Once your account arrives, the setup should be fairly mechanical.

TiviLive's current installation guide uses the Downloader app and its TiviLive app. After installation, you open the app and enter the credentials from your welcome email.

You can use IPTV Smarters Pro and select **Login with Xtream Codes API**, then enter your profile name, username, password and server URL. The guide says the channels and EPG should load after adding the user.

The guide lists IPTV Smarters Pro and other compatible players. The basic process is the same: install the player, select Xtream Codes, and enter the credentials supplied by the provider.

### On Samsung or LG Smart TVs

Install a compatible IPTV player from the TV's app store, then use either Xtream Codes credentials or an M3U playlist, depending on the player. TiviLive's guide also notes that some apps may ask for the TV's MAC address.

### On Windows or Mac

TiviLive currently lists MyIPTV Player, IPTV Smarters and VLC among the options. With an M3U playlist, VLC can also open the network stream directly.

## A Few Things to Check Before You Start Watching

There are three small details worth checking before blaming the IPTV service for every spinning loading icon.

First, copy the login credentials carefully. TiviLive specifically notes that even an extra space in the username, password or server URL can prevent a successful login.

Second, check your connection speed. The installation guide lists 15 Mbps as a starting point and 25 Mbps or more for 4K/UHD.

Third, test the service with the channels and viewing times that matter to you. A service can look perfect while playing a quiet movie at 2 p.m. and behave differently when everyone in the house is streaming during a major live event.
